1. Decision trees

A decision tree is a flow chart of yes/no questions. You start at the top and answer one question about your data at each step until you land on a leaf that holds the prediction.

PartWhat it is
Internal nodea test on one feature (for example "Outlook = ?")
Branchone outcome of the test
Leafthe predicted class

1.1 How the tree decides which question to ask first

Good questions split the data into groups that are as pure as possible (mostly one class). The tree is built top-down: pick the best question, split, then repeat inside each group. The notes use three ways to score a question.

Entropy: a number for "how mixed is this group?"

Entropy E(S) = − Σ p_c · log2(p_c) (sum over the classes c) Two classes: E = − p+ · log2(p+) − p− · log2(p−)

Information gain: how much does a question reduce the mess?

Gain(S, A) = E(S) − Σ ( |S_v| / |S| ) · E(S_v) before weighted entropy after splitting on A

The attribute with the largest gain goes first. Here it is, step by step, for the notes' weather data (9 Yes, 5 No):

  1. Entropy before: p(Yes) = 9/14, p(No) = 5/14. E = −(9/14)·log2(9/14) − (5/14)·log2(5/14) = 0.940.
  2. Split on Outlook: sunny has 5 days (2 Yes, 3 No), entropy 0.971. Overcast has 4 days (4 Yes, 0 No), entropy 0. Rainy has 5 days (3 Yes, 2 No), entropy 0.971.
  3. Weighted entropy after: (5/14)·0.971 + (4/14)·0 + (5/14)·0.971 = 0.693.
  4. Gain = 0.940 − 0.693 = 0.247.
AttributeGain
Outlook0.247 (largest, becomes the root)
Humidity0.152
Windy0.048
Temperature0.029

Then the tree repeats the process inside each branch. Under "sunny", Humidity has the largest gain (0.970), which is why it appears there. Under "rainy" the split is Windy.

Two refinements

Problem or variantWhat happensFix or alternative
Information gain favours attributes with many values (extreme case: an ID code that splits into 14 pure single-row leaves)Gain looks perfect but the tree is useless on new data (overfitting).Gain ratio (C4.5) divides gain by the "intrinsic information" of the split, which punishes many small branches.
Another purity scoreGini = 1 − Σ p_c². Pure group = 0. The split with the smallest weighted Gini wins.CART uses Gini and makes binary splits only.

Continuous features (like PageValue): sort the values, try the midpoint between each adjacent pair as a cut, and keep the cut with the highest gain. The result is a test like "PageValue < 1".

1.2 Overfitting and pruning

If you let a tree grow until every leaf is pure, it memorises the noise. Two cures:

  • Pre-pruning (early stopping): stop growing when a rule is hit: max_depth, min_samples_split (smallest group still allowed to split), min_samples_leaf (smallest allowed leaf).
  • Post-pruning: grow the full tree, then cut back branches that don't help on validation data.
ProsCons
Easy to read and explain. Little data preparation (no scaling needed). Shows which features matter most.Greedy, so not guaranteed to be the best tree. Unstable (small data changes can change the tree). Overfits when deep. Weaker with many classes and small data.

1.3 Reading a tree: the sample paper's Q1a

What it tells you. Buyers are sessions where visited pages have an economic value of at least 1 (PageValue) and an extremely low bounce rate. Sessions with low PageValue (78% of all) are almost never purchases.

Link to the business. The bookstore wants to target likely buyers with time-limited coupons. These two rules give a simple, explainable way to pick whom to target, and PageValue says which kind of browsing behaviour precedes a purchase.

A problem with the model. It is built on imbalanced data, and only 10% of sessions are predicted as buyers while 16% actually buy, so it misses buyers. Also the second leaf is only 74% pure, and the tree uses just two features. Fix: rebalance the training data (SMOTE or class weights), tune depth, or move to an ensemble.

How to judge the improved model. Evaluate on the untouched test set with metrics tied to the business: recall and precision of the buyer class, or better, the extra revenue minus coupon cost from targeting the predicted buyers.

Exam angleFor a tree question, answer in four moves: read the path to the "buy" leaf, state the business meaning, name one flaw (imbalance, shallow, overfit), and say how you would measure the improvement on held-out data.
Check yourself: why does information gain pick Outlook for the weather data?

Splitting on Outlook gives the biggest reduction in entropy (0.940 down to 0.693, gain 0.247). One branch (overcast) becomes perfectly pure, and the other two are less mixed than the whole.

2. Neural networks (NN)

A neural network is built from many tiny units called neurons, loosely inspired by the brain. It learns patterns by adjusting numbers (weights) until its answers match the labels.

2.1 One neuron

This is the same maths as logistic regression, with the sigmoid as f. A network is many such units stacked in layers.

2.2 Activation functions: why the bend matters

Without f, stacking layers would still only give a straight-line model. The bend lets the network learn curved boundaries.

FunctionFormulaTypical use
Sigmoid1 / (1 + e−x)output layer for a two-class problem (a probability)
Tanh(ex − e−x) / (ex + e−x)hidden layers in older networks
ReLUmax(0, x)hidden layers in modern and deep networks
Softmaxex_i / Σ ex_joutput layer for many classes (probabilities that sum to 1)

Softmax is not on the Day 2 slides but you will need it for the CNN question (Q2a asks for the classifier's activation function).

2.3 The multilayer perceptron (MLP)

  • Nodes in the first hidden layer each draw a straight cut through the feature space. The next layer combines those cuts into curved, complex regions.
  • More nodes and layers give more power, but need more data and training time, and overfit more easily.

2.4 How it learns: backpropagation and gradient descent

Training is a loop: guess, measure the error, nudge the weights to reduce it, repeat.

  1. Start with small random weights.
  2. Push a training example forward and get an output.
  3. Compute the error against the true label with a loss function (mean squared error for numbers, cross-entropy for classes).
  4. Send the error backward through the layers (backpropagation) to find how much each weight contributed.
  5. Adjust every weight a little in the direction that reduces the error (gradient descent).
  6. Repeat until the error is small enough or you hit a stopping rule.

2.5 Overtraining and early stopping

  • Prepare the data: scale inputs (min-max or z-score) and encode categories. Networks are sensitive to feature scale.
  • Hyperparameters to tune: number of hidden layers and nodes, learning rate, number of training cycles, batch size, dropout rate.
  • Strengths: very flexible, no assumptions about the data, handles numeric and categorical targets, very accurate.
  • Weaknesses: needs lots of data, slow to train, a "black box" that is hard to explain.
Exam angleQ1b says "one hidden layer with 10 hidden nodes". A typical follow-up asks you to say the network may have been trained on unscaled features, or overfitted, or lacks interpretability. Always tie the model to the business: can the bookstore explain why a customer was targeted?

3. Support vector machines (SVM)

An SVM separates two classes with a boundary, and chooses the boundary that leaves the widest possible gap between the classes. A wide gap means the model is less sensitive to small changes and tends to generalise better.

  • The boundary is the set of points where w·x + b = 0. Class +1 has w·x + b ≥ +1 and class −1 has w·x + b ≤ −1.
  • A bigger margin means a smaller ‖w‖, so training minimises ½ wᵀw subject to every point being on the right side.
  • The model is stored only as the support vectors, so a model with few support vectors generalises well, even with many features.

3.1 When the classes overlap: the soft margin and C

Real data rarely separates perfectly. A soft margin allows some points to sit inside the margin or on the wrong side, each paying a penalty (a slack variable). The setting C controls the price of those mistakes.

C is...MarginTraining errorsRisk
SmallWide (tolerant)More allowedUnderfitting, too simple
LargeNarrow (strict)Fewer allowedOverfitting, follows noise
C → ∞Hard marginNone allowedOnly works if the data is perfectly separable

3.2 When a straight line is not enough: kernels

If no straight boundary works, map the data into a higher-dimensional space where one does. The notes' example has five points on a line: class A at 1, 2, 6 and class B at 4, 5. No single cut on the line separates them, because B sits in the middle of A.

You never have to compute the mapping directly. A kernel function gives the dot product in the high-dimensional space directly, the "kernel trick". Common choices:

KernelBoundary shapeSettings
Linearstraight line or planeC
Polynomial, e.g. (xi·xj + 1)²curved, up to the chosen degreeC, degree
RBF (Gaussian)flexible, local blobsC, gamma (large gamma = tighter blobs, overfits)

More than two classes: one-vs-rest (one SVM per class, take the highest score) or one-vs-one (one SVM per pair, majority vote).

In practice: scale the features, pick the kernel, tune C (and gamma) on validation data, then check on the test set.

ProsCons
High accuracy, handles non-linear boundaries, works well in high dimensions, depends only on the support vectors.Slow on very large data, needs scaling and tuning (C, kernel, gamma), no direct probabilities, hard to interpret.

5. Practice questions (exam style)

Question A: choose the first split

A bookstore has 10 sessions: 4 ended in a purchase and 6 did not. Two candidate first questions are tested.

QuestionAnswer = yesAnswer = no
1. Viewed 5 or more product pages?5 sessions: 4 buy, 1 no buy5 sessions: 0 buy, 5 no buy
2. Weekend visit?4 sessions: 2 buy, 2 no buy6 sessions: 2 buy, 4 no buy

(a) Calculate the information gain of each question and say which one the tree should use first. Hint: E(0.5) = 1, E(0.8) = 0.722, E(1/3) = 0.918, E(0.4) = 0.971. (b) Name one way to stop the finished tree from overfitting. (4 marks)

Show the worked answer

Entropy before: p(buy) = 0.4, so E = 0.971.

Question 1: yes group (4 of 5 buy) has E = 0.722, no group (pure) has E = 0. Weighted = (5/10)·0.722 + (5/10)·0 = 0.361. Gain = 0.971 − 0.361 = 0.610.

Question 2: yes group (2 of 4) has E = 1, no group (2 of 6) has E = 0.918. Weighted = (4/10)·1 + (6/10)·0.918 = 0.951. Gain = 0.971 − 0.951 = 0.020.

Decision: use Question 1 (product pages) first, because its gain is far larger. The weekend question barely changes the mix.

(b) Limit the depth or the minimum samples per leaf (pre-pruning), or grow the full tree and prune branches that do not improve validation accuracy (post-pruning).

Question B: diagnose a model

An SVM with an RBF kernel, C = 1000 and a large gamma gets 100% training accuracy and 72% test accuracy on the bookstore data. (a) What has happened? (b) Give two changes. (c) The same company would prefer a model its marketing team can read. Which of the three Day 2 models fits and why? (4 marks)

Show the worked answer

(a) Overfitting. A very large C allows almost no training errors, and a large gamma makes tight local boundaries around individual points, so the model has memorised the training set.

(b) Lower C (wider, softer margin), lower gamma (smoother boundary), or use a simpler kernel. Choose values by grid search with cross-validation on the training data, then confirm on the test set.

(c) A decision tree. Each prediction is a short chain of readable rules, such as "PageValue at least 1 and a very low bounce rate", which marketing can turn directly into a targeting policy.

Quick recall questions
  1. What does a leaf node hold? What does an internal node hold?
  2. High entropy means high or low uncertainty?
  3. Why is an ID-code attribute a bad split even though its information gain is the highest?
  4. What is backpropagation doing, in one sentence?
  5. In an SVM, what are support vectors?
  6. What does raising C do?

Answers: (1) A leaf holds a class label, an internal node holds a test on one feature. (2) High. (3) It splits into single-row pure leaves, which memorise the data and do not generalise (gain ratio corrects this). (4) It sends the output error backwards through the layers to tell each weight how to change. (5) The training points closest to the boundary, which alone define the margin. (6) Penalises mistakes more, giving a narrower margin and more risk of overfitting.
